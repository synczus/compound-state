## 2. Revised Position: Stimulant Therapy (Adderall)

**Original text (v1.0):**
"Traditional Stimulants: Avoid unless clinician strongly determines otherwise, due to dopamine spikes and psychosis relapse concern."

**Amended text (v1.1):**
Traditional Stimulants (Adderall, mixed amphetamine salts) should remain on the table as a clinically valid option. The patient reports that Adderall is the single most effective intervention for concentration, task initiation, and reducing attention fragmentation during conversation. The psychosis relapse concern is acknowledged and taken seriously. The requested approach is a risk-mitigated protocol rather than a blanket exclusion.

**Proposed risk-mitigated protocol:**
- Start at 5mg IR or 10mg XR — lowest effective dose
- Pair with an alpha-2 agonist (Guanfacine ER 1mg or Clonidine) to dampen noradrenergic side effects and reduce physical agitation
- PRN-only schedule — not daily; used only on days requiring sustained focus or social interaction
- Self-track sleep, hypermonitoring intensity, and any perceptual changes
- If sleep drops below 6 hours or any perceptual instability appears, pause immediately and review

**Rationale:**
The initial psychotic episode was triggered by a high-dose serotonergic psychedelic (DMT), not stimulant accumulation. The patient has used Adderall post-episode without relapse. Current functional impairment from attention deficits and hypermonitoring is severe enough that the risk-benefit calculus favors a controlled trial over continued functional decline.

## 3. Clinical Rationale

**Psychosis relapse risk:** The episode was DMT-triggered, not stimulant-triggered. The patient maintained stability through prior Adderall use without relapse.

**Dopamine spike concern:** Mitigated by low-dose protocol paired with an alpha-2 agonist co-prescription and a PRN-only schedule.

**Hypermonitoring amplification:** Lower-dose stimulants can reduce monitoring load by improving attention filtering rather than amplifying it.

**Safer alternatives tried:** Guanfacine alone has been insufficient. Non-stimulant options do not match the functional need.
